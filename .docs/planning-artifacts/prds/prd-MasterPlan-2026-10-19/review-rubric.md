# PRD Quality Review — MasterPlan

## Overall verdict
A focused, honest PRD that holds up well for a graded solo project. The thesis ("every task has someone responsible for it, and nothing is forgotten without someone noticing") runs from the Vision through the Glossary and status rules to the counter-metrics. Most FRs carry real, testable consequences, and the Test Mode section is unusually concrete. Two problems remain. The Task status model has no state for a task that has no helper, and the PRD quietly assumes local-only running while its own friends test (SM-1 to SM-5) needs helpers on phones and real email. A few smaller done-ness gaps (organizer and event names, the "seen" rule for replies, late acceptances in FR-17) will reach story creation as ambiguity unless they are fixed now.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions, with what was given up made clear. Unanswered tasks get "no automatic reminder" (UJ-2 step 7, §5.6). Reminders are fixed text, and AI-written ones move to Stage 2 (§8). "Real-time push is not required" (FR-22). Past deadlines are clamped to today and do not trigger the fallback (FR-5). The Vision admits "It has no technical moat." Open Questions 3–6 are genuinely open and small enough to settle during UX or architecture.

The weak point is Open Question 2 ("does v1 only ever run locally, or is it also deployed"). It is filed as a neutral question, but the Product success metrics depend on its answer. In §9, 2–3 friends act as helpers in a realistic test event. In UJ-2, Ola taps a link sent on Messenger from his phone. Neither works against a laptop's localhost, and SM-2/SM-3 need real email delivery (Open Question 1) too. The 14-day reminder rule also may never fire inside a test window that ends before the November deadline. This tension is real, and the PRD does not surface it.

### Findings
- **[high]** Friends test depends on unresolved hosting and email (§10 Q2, Q1; §9 Product; UJ-2 step 1) — SM-1 to SM-5 assume helpers open links on their phones and receive reminder emails. With local-only running, neither happens. The window before the November deadline may also be shorter than the 14-day reminder interval (FR-17). *Fix:* decide Q2 in the PRD, or reframe it as a tension: either "v1 is deployed on a free host for the friends test" or "the friends test runs on a shared LAN/tunnel with compressed deadlines". State whether the test event uses short deadlines so the 3-day reminder rule is the one exercised.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is no furniture. The three users (§2) each drive requirements: the Organizer drives FR-1 to FR-22, the Helper drives FR-12 to FR-18 and NFR-1, and the Examiner drives all of §6. The Vision is specific to this product and could not be swapped into a generic task-app PRD ("doesn't want a project-management tool", "nobody follows up"). The NFRs are product-specific: NFR-3 ("Helper emails are never sent to the AI"), NFR-4 (zero running cost) and NFR-7 (no humor, "which could read as passive-aggressive"). FR-12's line "Chat apps load links in the background to build previews" is a good example of an earned rationale. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is accountability and follow-through for non-planners, and the features serve it. The AI exists only to remove the blank-board problem (FR-5, FR-6). Assignment is to exactly one helper (FR-9). The status vocabulary (Urgent / Needs a helper / On track) exists so the organizer notices in time. Reminders target only accepted work. The MVP is clearly problem-solving in kind, and the Stage 2 / Out-of-scope lists (§8) cut along the thesis (budget, RSVP, voting boards all excluded). The build order in §8 follows dependencies, which is reasonable. The counter-metrics are well chosen. CM-2 ("first shows as Needs a helper within 3 days of its deadline, too late to reassign") directly tests the "early enough" promise.

### Findings
- **[medium]** SM-4 and SM-5 have no measurement (§9) — SM-4 ("only adjusts the AI plan draft. She does not rewrite it") has no threshold of its own; the only number lives in CM-1 (">half"). SM-5 ("helpers find the reminders helpful, not annoying") does not say how this is collected. With n = 2–3 helpers, the reflection report needs a stated method. *Fix:* give SM-4 its own threshold (e.g. "≤25% of drafted tasks deleted or retitled"). Define SM-5 as a 1–2 question post-test survey with a pass bar.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs are testable, and several are excellent. FR-17 has worked examples, catch-up and no-duplicate rules. FR-6 gives an explicit invalid-plan definition. FR-12 sets a 128-bit token and requires that opening a link never changes data. FR-21 recalculates status on every page load. FR-25 and FR-19 tie seed data to expected counts. The gaps below are the kind that become arguments during story creation.

### Findings
- **[high]** No status for a task without a helper (§4 Glossary "Task status", "Task"; FR-5, FR-9, FR-21) — A Task has "at most one assigned helper", but the five statuses are "exactly one of" and Open is defined as "assigned, not yet answered". Freshly drafted tasks (UJ-1 step 5) and tasks the organizer takes over herself (UJ-3 step 4) have no defined status. It is also unclear whether they count toward Needs a helper, On track or "N of M done". The same applies to a Declined task: does it stay assigned to, and visible on the page of, the helper who declined it? This hits the core promise (SM-1 "no task is silently forgotten"). *Fix:* add an **Unassigned** status (or define Open to include unassigned). State that Unassigned counts as Needs a helper. State whether Declined / Can't be done tasks stay on the original helper's page until reassigned.
- **[medium]** Organizer name and event name are used but never captured (FR-1, FR-3, FR-18; UJ-2 step 1; Glossary "Event") — FR-1 collects only email and password, yet FR-18 needs "[organizer name]" for the reminder text and sender, and the helper page header reads "Kari has invited you…". The Glossary's Event has date, location, guest count and general information but no name, yet the tabs and header show "Wedding" and "Bachelorette Party". *Fix:* add a display name to FR-1. Add an event name (defaulting to the template name) to FR-3 and the Glossary.
- **[medium]** FR-17 has no rule for late acceptances (FR-17) — A task accepted fewer than 3 days before its deadline, or after it, has a "3 days before" moment already in the past. It is unclear whether it is reminded at once on the next check or never. FR-14's restarted clock has the same edge. *Fix:* add a consequence, e.g. "If the 3-days-before time has already passed at acceptance, no reminder is sent" (or "sent at the next check").
- **[medium]** "New reply … not yet seen" has no defined seen event (Glossary "New reply"; FR-13, FR-14, FR-20 Messages) — Loading the event view, expanding the Messages panel, or an explicit action could each count as "seen". Each gives different counts and tests. *Fix:* state the rule, e.g. "A reply counts as seen once the organizer has expanded the Messages panel of that event after it arrived."
- **[low]** FRs with no or tautological consequences (FR-7, FR-9, FR-16) — FR-9 and FR-16 have no consequence bullet. FR-7's bullet restates the requirement. *Fix:* e.g. FR-9 "an assigned task appears on that helper's link as Open"; FR-16 "a task marked Done by the organizer disappears from the helper's reminder link and gets no reminder".
- **[low]** Adjectives where a bound is cheap (NFR-1 "every button is easy to tap"; FR-18 / NFR-7 "respectful and direct") — *Fix:* NFR-1: minimum 44×44 px tap targets. FR-18: put the fixed reminder text in the docs so "respectful" is reviewed once rather than tested.

## Scope honesty — adequate
Omissions are mostly explicit. §2 "Not for v1", the §8 Out-of-scope and Stage 2 lists, and FR-22's "Real-time push is not required" do real work. There is one inline `[ASSUMPTION]` (NFR-6), which is appropriate density for these stakes. The silent assumption is local-only running, which shapes test mode.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Test mode switches on implicitly, and its pages ignore data isolation (FR-23, FR-24, FR-26; NFR-2; FR-2) — "When no language-model key is configured, the app runs in test mode", and the test inbox "lists every email the app would have sent". That includes every helper link, across all organizers. On a deployed instance without an AI key (Open Q2 / Q1 not yet decided), this exposes other organizers' helper links and breaks FR-2. Linking test mode to the AI key also leaves unclear what happens when the AI key is set but email credentials are not. *Fix:* state that v1 test mode is for local use only, or require the explicit `.env` flag for test mode, scope the test inbox to the logged-in organizer, and define behavior for each missing credential separately.

## Downstream usability — strong
The Glossary is strong and mostly held to: Urgent / Needs a helper / On track are disjoint and precisely defined. FR, NFR, UJ, SM and CM IDs are contiguous and unique, and cross-references (FR-6, FR-17, FR-25, FR-26, NFR-7) resolve. Each UJ has a named protagonist and a device context. The addendum cleanly separates UX and tech notes for bmad-ux and bmad-architecture.

### Findings
- **[low]** "Updates at once" vs "next time they load" (UJ-2 steps 3 and 6 "Kari's overview updates at once"; FR-22 titled "Live updates") — The FR rejects push, but the journey text and the FR title imply it. A UX or story writer may build polling. *Fix:* rename FR-22 to "Fresh on load", and change the UJ-2 wording to "the next time Kari opens her overview, it shows…".

## Shape fit — strong
A journey-led shape suits this product: two roles on different devices, with the value in the handoff (link → answer → overview), plus an examiner whose journey (UJ-4) maps directly to the course's grading. The separate §6 Test Mode is a good fit for the stakes. At about 5–6 pages, the PRD hits the compact target, and the rigor is proportionate. There is no over-formalization: no persona sprawl, and no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts added for show. No findings.

## Mechanical notes
- §0 still says "FR-1 to FR-N"; it should be "FR-1 to FR-28".
- §0 promises "Inline `[ASSUMPTION: …]` tags", but there is no Assumptions Index. With one tag (NFR-6) that is acceptable. Either add a one-line index or drop the plural framing.
- Glossary drift: "Needs a helper" vs "tasks that Need a helper" (FR-19, FR-20) vs "2 needs a helper" (UJ-3, addendum); "Bachelorette Party" (UJ-2 header, addendum) vs "Bachelorette party" (Glossary). The panel is "Messages" in FR-20 but "Messages / helpers" in the addendum.
- "Helper page" is used throughout (FR-11, FR-12, NFR-1, §5.5 title) but is not a Glossary term. Only "Helper link" is defined. Add it, or define it as "the page a helper link opens".
- "Fixture table" (FR-20, FR-25) and "traceability table" (FR-28, §9) are referenced as docs artifacts but have no location. Naming the file (e.g. `docs/fixtures.md`) would help stories.
